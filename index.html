SIH PS — Dataset + Limitations Map

N1

Road Accident Black-Spot & Severity Prediction Using Multi-Source Crash + Infrastructure + Weather Data

Problem: India has ~1% of the world's vehicles but ~11% of global road accident deaths (~1.7 lakh fatalities/year). Black-spot identification is largely manual and reactive, often happening after clusters of deaths are reported.

Data Sources
Data TypeSourceAccess MethodNotes
Road Accidents in India (annual report)MoRTHFree download (PDF/data.gov.in)State/district-wise cause, severity, vehicle type, time of day.
Accidental Deaths & Suicides in India (ADSI)NCRBFree downloadComplements MoRTH with cause-coded fatality data.
State/year-wise cases, injured, deathsDataful / data.gov.in (sourced from NCRB)Free downloadCleaned tabular version of NCRB data.
Kaggle India Road Accident DatasetKaggleFree downloadState, year, severity, vehicle type — useful for prototyping before raw government data.
Road network & geometryOpenStreetMapFree via Overpass APICurvature, lane count, junction density, road class.
Weather at time of accidentIMDFree downloadRainfall, visibility, fog — correlates strongly with severity.
Traffic volumeGoogle/HERE traffic APIs; city traffic police open dataFree tier / partialSparse outside major metros.
Vehicle registration densityVahan Dashboard (MoRTH)Free downloadDistrict-wise vehicle population; rough exposure denominator.
Speed limit / signage inventoryState PWD / Highway Authority GIS portalsVaries by stateUseful but inconsistent coverage.
Limitations
  1. Location Imprecision — Older MoRTH/NCRB records often use landmark-based location, not GPS; historical black-spot geocoding is noisy.
  2. No Exposure Denominator — Raw accident counts do not account for traffic volume per segment; severity-per-vehicle cannot be computed reliably without volume data.
  3. Underreporting — Minor injury accidents are believed to be significantly underreported relative to fatal ones, biasing models toward severe-only patterns.
  4. State-Level Data Fragmentation — Reporting formats and quality vary by state and police station, complicating a pan-India model.
  5. Causal Confounds — Enforcement intensity, ambulance response time, and hospital proximity affect fatality outcomes independently of crash mechanics and are rarely available jointly.
N2

Hyperlocal Air Quality Nowcasting for Underserved Wards Using CAAQMS + Satellite + Low-Cost Sensor Fusion

Problem: India's official CAAQMS network has only ~500 continuous stations for 4,000+ towns/cities, leaving many wards—especially poorer, denser ones—without ground-truth AQI and dependent on city-average values.

Data Sources
Data TypeSourceAccess MethodNotes
Real-time station AQI (all pollutants)CPCB CAAQMSFree, advance-search download per station15-min to daily granularity; ~500+ stations nationally.
AQI APIdata.gov.in (CPCB resource)Free API keyGood for building a live pipeline.
Historical/manual monitoring (NAMP)CPCB via data.gov.inFree downloadOlder, sparser, pre-2015-heavy.
Aggregated multi-source AQIOpenAQFree API, S3 bulk archiveHarmonized CPCB + global data; easy single entry point.
Aerosol Optical Depth / PM proxyNASA MODIS, Sentinel-5P via Google Earth EngineFreeSatellite-derived pollution proxy for ungauged wards.
Land use / built-up densityISRO Bhuvan LULCFree downloadRoads, industrial zones, green cover per ward.
Traffic congestionCity traffic police portals / crowdsourced appsVariesPatchy outside metros; optional feature.
Meteorology (wind, inversion, humidity)IMDFree downloadDispersion strongly modulates local PM levels.
Ward boundariesSurvey of India / municipal GIS portalsVaries by cityNeeded to aggregate predictions to an actionable unit.
Limitations
  1. Sparse Ground Truth Outside Metros — Tier-2/3 cities and many wards within large cities have zero calibration points, so the model is extrapolated where it is most needed.
  2. Satellite–Surface Mismatch — AOD measures the whole atmospheric column, not ground-level concentration; converting it to surface PM2.5 needs uncertain boundary-layer-height correction.
  3. Low-Cost Sensor Drift — Crowdsourced/DIY sensors drift and need frequent co-location calibration against CPCB reference stations.
  4. Download Throttling — CPCB's portal limits historical bulk downloads to short windows per request, making multi-year collection an engineering bottleneck.
  5. Seasonal Confounding — Stubble-burning, Diwali, and monsoon washout create large non-stationary swings that may hurt cross-season generalization.
N3

Cash-Flow-Based MSME Credit Risk Scoring Using GST Filing Patterns + Alternative Data

Problem: India has ~6.3 crore MSMEs contributing ~30% of GDP, but only 10–15% have access to formal credit. Many are thin-file businesses without audited financials and are rejected by collateral-based underwriting.

Data Sources
Data TypeSourceAccess MethodNotes
MSME registration & classificationUdyam Registration PortalAggregated stats free; record-level not public~3.2 crore registered MSMEs, ~95% micro.
GST return structure & schemaGSTN developer/API documentationPublic documentationReal filer-level data is private; use schema to generate realistic synthetic returns.
Synthetic/sample credit scoring datasetsKaggle (SME/loan default datasets), UCIFree downloadProxy for feature engineering and model-validation logic.
TReDS invoice financing volumesRXIL / M1xchange / Invoicemart public disclosuresFree (aggregate reports)Sector/scale context, not record-level.
MSME sector employment & outputMinistry of MSME Annual ReportFree downloadSector-wise benchmarks to sanity-check model outputs.
Formal credit gap & NPA benchmarksRBI / SIDBI reportsFree downloadUseful for setting risk thresholds and validating against known default rates.
Business compliance signalsGST portal public search (GSTIN status)Free, per-GSTIN lookupBinary compliance-health feature; not bulk-downloadable.
Digital payment activity (proxy)RBI Digital Payments Index, NPCI UPI statisticsFree download (aggregated)City/sector level only; macro control variable, not firm-level.
Limitations
  1. No Public Firm-Level Ground Truth — Actual GST filings and default outcomes are private/regulated; a hackathon build must use synthetic data, limiting confidence in real-world accuracy claims.
  2. Survivorship Bias — Obtainable MSME success datasets skew toward formal, Udyam-registered businesses; informal micro-enterprises are least represented.
  3. Label Definition Ambiguity — 'Default' and 'creditworthy' differ across NBFCs, banks, and TReDS financiers; without a standard label, models may optimize an arbitrary proxy.
  4. Regulatory Sensitivity — Real deployment would require Account Aggregator / RBI data-sharing consent frameworks; a hackathon prototype can only demonstrate the pipeline.
  5. Seasonal & Sector Cash-Flow Variance — Festive retail and monsoon-dependent businesses can be misclassified when short GST histories miss normal seasonality.
N4

Public Bus Bunching & ETA Reliability Prediction Using GTFS + Real-Time Feeds

Problem: Bus commuters in many Indian cities face unpredictable wait times because buses bunch into clusters with long gaps, while app-shown ETAs can be frequently wrong.

Data Sources
Data TypeSourceAccess MethodNotes
Static GTFS (routes, stops, schedule)Delhi Open Transit Data (otd.delhi.gov.in)Free downloadIndia's most mature open bus GTFS + real-time feed.
Static GTFS, other citiesChartr / busmaps.com aggregatorFree downloadGrowing but uneven city coverage; freshness must be checked.
Real-time vehicle positions (GTFS-RT)Delhi OTD real-time APIFree APINeeded to compute actual vs scheduled headways—the bunching signal.
Global GTFS/GTFS-RT catalogMobility DatabaseFree, searchableCross-check which Indian cities have a live feed.
Road traffic conditionsOpenStreetMap + city traffic data where availableFree / partialExplains why a route is bunching (congestion segment).
WeatherIMDFree downloadRain correlates with bunching through slower boarding/traffic.
Ridership / stop-level demand (proxy)Smart card / ticketing open dashboards where publishedVariesRarely public at stop level; often a binding constraint.
Limitations
  1. Live Feed Coverage Is Thin — Only a handful of Indian cities currently publish genuinely live, well-maintained GTFS-RT feeds.
  2. Static Schedule ≠ Operational Reality — Published GTFS timetables may not match actual operations, so schedule-vs-actual gaps can reflect stale GTFS.
  3. No Stop-Level Demand Data — Without boarding/alighting counts, the model can predict irregularity but not rider impact.
  4. GPS Noise in Dense Traffic — Urban-canyon effects and traffic-jam idling create noisy traces requiring substantial cleaning.
  5. Single-City External Validity — A model tuned to Delhi may not transfer to a smaller city without local retraining.
S1

On-Spot RTRWH & Artificial Recharge Assessment App

Problem: There is no easy way for a citizen or engineer to estimate rooftop rainwater-harvesting potential on the spot and size the appropriate artificial-recharge structure for a plot.

Data Sources
Data TypeSourceAccess MethodNotes
Rainfall DataIMD Gridded RainfallFree download0.25° resolution, daily.
Groundwater LevelsCGWB / India-WRISFree downloadWell-level, pre/post-monsoon.
Aquifer MapsCGWB NAQUIMFree download3D aquifer geometry, 500+ districts.
Soil DataNBSS&LUP Soil MapsResearch collaboration1:250,000 scale; coarse for plot-level use.
Building FootprintsMicrosoft Building Footprints (India) / satellite imageryFree downloadVector data for rooftop area estimation.
Land Use/CoverISRO Bhuvan LULCFree downloadUpdated every 5 years.
Design StandardsCGWB Master Plan for Artificial Recharge; CPHEEO manualFree downloadRunoff coefficients and structure-sizing norms.
Real-time Recharge DataINGRESFree via portalSystem this PS is meant to feed.
Existing RTRWH InventoryState Groundwater Boards / ULBsVaries, often proprietaryRarely digitized.
Limitations
  1. Rooftop Estimation Accuracy — Building-footprint data is coarse in dense old-city fabric; no verified national plot-level rooftop-area dataset.
  2. Aquifer Heterogeneity — Alluvial vs hard-rock response varies street to street; regional parameters do not generalize to one plot.
  3. Soil/Geology Granularity — NBSS&LUP maps are too coarse for building-scale infiltration-rate estimates.
  4. No RTRWH Ground Truth — Most existing structures are undocumented, leaving little ground truth for recharge validation.
  5. Fragmented Ownership — CGWB, State Groundwater Boards, and ULBs hold pieces of the data in incompatible formats.
  6. Sparse DWLR Network — Real-time recharge validation needs a denser digital water-level-recorder network.
S2

AI-Based Rockfall Prediction & Alert System for Open-Pit Mines

Problem: Rockfalls in open-pit mines are a major cause of miner injury/death, but there is no scalable early-warning system that ties slope, weather, and blast data together.

Data Sources
Data TypeSourceAccess MethodNotes
Accident/Incident DataDGMS Annual ReportsFree download (aggregated)National-level, not per-mine or per-bench.
Lithology/Geology MapsGeological Survey of India — Bhukosh PortalFree downloadRegional scale.
Ground DeformationSentinel-1 InSAR (ESA Copernicus)Free downloadUseful for slow slope movement, not sudden rockfall.
Terrain/DEMCartosat / SRTM DEM via Bhuvan, USGSFree downloadFor slope angle/bench geometry.
Rainfall DataIMD Gridded + station dataFree downloadRain is a major rockfall trigger.
Seismic DataNational Center for SeismologyFree downloadBlast-induced and tectonic triggers.
Mine-specific Slope DataIndividual mine survey/monitoring systems (e.g. Coal India)Direct partnership, proprietaryRarely shared externally.
Published Slope-Stability DatasetsAcademic papers (mostly non-Indian mines)VariesSmall samples, different rock types.
Limitations
  1. No Public India-Specific Rockfall Database — Most usable datasets are proprietary or from non-Indian mines with different geology.
  2. Sparse Instrumentation — Real-time slope radar/extensometers exist at only a handful of large mines; no standardized deployment.
  3. Coarse Geology Maps — Regional GSI maps miss bench-level joint/fracture patterns that drive rockfall triggers.
  4. Missing Operational Data — Blasting logs and vibration records are rarely digitized or shared.
  5. Temporal Mismatch — Rainfall-triggered failures need sub-daily localized rainfall; IMD gridded data is coarser.
  6. Liability Reluctance — Mines may be cautious about sharing near-miss/failure data due to scrutiny and safety liability.
S3

AI-Powered Crop Yield Prediction & Optimization

Problem: Odisha's farmers and agriculture department lack reliable, timely yield forecasts for procurement, price support, and input-distribution planning.

Data Sources
Data TypeSourceAccess MethodNotes
Crop Production DataUPAg Portal (Ministry of Agriculture)Free downloadDistrict-wise, crop-wise.
State Agri StatisticsDirectorate of Agriculture & Food Production, OdishaFree/partial downloadState-specific.
Weather DataIMD Gridded Rainfall + Odisha AWS networkFree downloadDaily, station + gridded.
Soil HealthSoil Health Card PortalFree download (aggregated)Nutrient status by block.
Satellite Crop MonitoringISRO NRSC FASAL ProjectFree/research accessPurpose-built for India crop forecasting.
Vegetation IndicesMODIS/Sentinel-2 via Google Earth EngineFreeNDVI time series.
Irrigation Source DataUPAg / Minor Irrigation CensusFree downloadCanal/well/tank-wise.
Market PricesAgmarknetFree APIDaily wholesale prices.
Yield-Loss/Insurance DataPMFBY PortalPartial/aggregatedCrop insurance claims as a proxy for loss.
Limitations
  1. Sparse Ground Truth — Actual yield data from crop-cutting experiments is delayed by months and low-sample per district.
  2. Intra-State Variability — Odisha spans coastal, plateau, and hill agro-climatic zones; district aggregation hides this.
  3. Fragmented Landholdings — Many farms are under 1 hectare, so one satellite pixel can mix several farmers' plots.
  4. Monsoon Cloud Cover — Kharif overlaps with heavy cloud cover, degrading optical satellite availability when needed most.
  5. No Plot-Level History — Yield records are not tied to farmer identity over time, limiting farmer-specific supervised learning.
  6. Reporting Lag — Price and official yield data can lag real conditions by weeks to months.
S4

FloatChat — AI Conversational Interface for ARGO Ocean Data

Problem: ARGO ocean-float data on temperature, salinity, and depth is rich but locked behind technical formats, making it difficult for non-specialists such as fisherfolk, students, and policymakers to use.

Data Sources
Data TypeSourceAccess MethodNotes
ARGO Float ProfilesIndian Argo Project / INCOIS, international Argo GDACFree downloadTemperature/salinity vs depth; global network.
Ocean State ForecastsINCOISFree accessIndian Ocean-specific.
Satellite SST / Ocean ColorNASA MODIS / Oceansat via Bhuvan, ISROFree downloadSurface-level complement to ARGO depth profiles.
BathymetryGEBCO Global BathymetryFree downloadOcean-floor depth.
Cyclone/Storm TracksIMD RSMC New DelhiFree downloadFor event-linked queries.
Fisheries AdvisoriesINCOIS Potential Fishing Zone (PFZ) advisoriesFree accessDirect relevance to fisherfolk use case.
Marine BiodiversityOBISFree downloadFor biodiversity-related queries.
Limitations
  1. Sparse Coastal Coverage — ARGO floats operate mainly in open ocean deeper than ~1000m; shallow Indian coastal waters are under-sampled.
  2. No India-Specific Ocean NLP Corpus — There is no established domain training corpus mapping natural-language questions to oceanographic parameters.
  3. Data Latency & QC Variability — Real-time float data can carry provisional quality flags; fully quality-controlled delayed-mode data lags by months.
  4. Multilingual Gap — Regional-language support is needed, but oceanographic terminology is not well covered in Indian-language NLP resources.
  5. Cross-Dataset Alignment — Combining ARGO with satellite and fisheries data requires spatial-temporal interpolation, adding uncertainty.
S5

Indigenous Contactless Integrated Track Monitoring System (ITMS)

Problem: Indian railway-track condition recording still relies heavily on periodic Track Recording Car (TRC) runs, leaving no continuous contactless system for early defect detection between runs.

Data Sources
Data TypeSourceAccess MethodNotes
Track Maintenance StandardsRDSO manualsFree downloadDefines defect thresholds.
Historical TRC DataIndian Railways zonal recordsInternal/restrictedNot publicly released.
Track Defect HistoryIndian Railways Track Management System (TMS)InternalWould need direct partnership.
Rail Asset InventoryIndian Railways GIS asset managementInternalTrack/asset geolocation.
Comparable Open DatasetsKaggle rail-fault / vibration datasetsFree downloadNon-Indian gauge/rolling stock; limited transfer.
Weather DataIMDFree downloadTemperature swings drive rail-buckling risk.
Sensor Benchmark DataPublished accelerometer/vibration research datasetsFree (academic)Useful for sensor-fusion validation only.
Limitations
  1. No Public Indian Track-Defect Dataset — TMS and TRC data are internal; real labels would require direct partnership.
  2. Gauge/Loading Mismatch — Open rail-fault datasets use different gauges and axle loads, limiting transfer.
  3. Operating Envelope — Contactless sensing must work across India's speed range and monsoon conditions; no public dataset spans that combination.
  4. Expensive Ground Truth — Defect classification still needs expert Permanent-Way inspection, which is slow and costly to scale.
  5. Retrofitting Constraint — Combining 'contactless' and 'indigenous' rules out many off-the-shelf laser/optical systems.
